# BRIEF: Drift, a travel year read as a route

**Self-authored under explicit creative delegation.** No interview. Every decision is authored, not quoted. Evidence: `apps/client/src/pages/atlas/domains/content/drift.ts` (EN and AR copy, figures; all sample content), `base.ts` (paper #e9f5f1, ink #153f45, saffron #e6ae48, soft #f8edcb), `type.ts` (EN Bricolage Grotesque + Figtree, AR Alexandria), stills `drift-1` to `drift-4`. The hero film is unused on purpose (type-only title page). Nothing generated. The page says on its face that brand and figures are sample content. Source: `build.mjs` + `src/` generate both languages from one data object.

## The eight topics

1. **Vibe.** A printed itinerary and a train ticket. Seafoam paper, deep teal ink, one saffron. References from other media: a boarding pass, a railway timetable, a hand-inked route on a paper map.
2. **Journey.** Ticket in hand, five journeys in calendar order (Feb, May, Jun, Sep, Nov), the year summed, the hosts' field guide, one guest's journal, then a sentence to fill in.
3. **Energy curve.** Calm, steady turning of chapters, one long swell where the year draws itself, a quiet two chapters, a resolved close.
4. **Feeling curve and the ONE moment.** Below. The moment: the route line along the edge finishes drawing the year.
5. **What no other site does.** The route line on the edge is the nav and the meter: it fills as chapters pass, and a counter beside it adds up the days written on each journey you read (11, 7, 8, 9, 5).
6. **Range.** Editorial and warm. Hard-cut grounds, one flat accent, no motion beyond a clip wipe and the line.
7. **One world or distinct scenes.** Distinct chapters, hard cuts (chaptered editorial). No continuous film, no scrub, no pin.
8. **Assets.** Project stills only (`drift-1`, `-2`, `-3`, `-4`), each captioned as a sample image, not the place. No film.

Subject: Drift is a slow-travel club (fictional). The visitor must believe the days feel unplanned because the routes are slow, local and few. One action, one label everywhere: **Plan a drift** (AR: خطط لرحلة).

## The peak

What a visitor would say: *"I read a year of trips and a line along the edge drew the whole slow route, adding up to forty days."* Lives in stop 5 (the year plate), the largest span on the page (about 2 viewport heights; the page is about 11.5). Stop 4 is the open, quieter lead-in.

## Tell-someone sentence

It's the site where the line along the edge draws your whole travel year as you read it, and counts the days.

## Authored silence

The hero (p 0) and the first third of the close are still by design. `data-sc-verify-state` on the route nav reports chapter, fill and day count on every scroll position, so nothing reads as dead.

## Why the other seven grammars lost

- Filmic one-shot: one 4 s loop would be stretched across five different trips; the content is a set of separate journeys, not one arc.
- Live surface: no state to operate honestly; a fake booking tool would break the sample-content rule.
- Continuous world: no flight assets exist, and the trips are distinct places.
- Typographic poster: the copy is long and factual, not one claim.
- Gallery / catalog: the first-guess fit (five journeys), but the argument is sequence and time, not browsing a collection.
- Split stage: no two-sided argument.
- Rhythmic cutlist: the tone is slow; a cut a second would contradict the brand.
