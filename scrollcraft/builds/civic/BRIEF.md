## The eight topics

1. **Vibe.** Clear, plain, municipal. References: a subway map, a before/after photo slider, a stopwatch.
2. **Journey.** Two readings of one city (Today, With the toolkit), the hardest neighbourhood, the middle one, the one with a documented before, then the improved column takes the page and asks for one action.
3. **Energy curve.** Calm, steady, quieter, one long fall, resolved stop.
4. **Feeling curve and the ONE moment.** Below. The moment: you drag the line across the city and the wait times fall under your finger.
5. **What no other site does.** A draggable divider (mouse, touch, keyboard) whose tab names the neighbourhood under it and shows its measured minutes, a bar from the real value, and for Eastgate the documented before (31). Scroll moves it too.
6. **Range.** Premium-minimal, municipal-plain. Not brutalist, not playful.
7. **One world or distinct scenes.** Neither. One held stage (Split stage) over four short spacer acts.
8. **Assets.** Owned: the real figures in `civic.ts`. Everything visual is SVG built by the page.

Subject: Civic makes public services easier to find. The visitor must believe the difference is measurable in minutes. One action, one label everywhere: **Work with us** (AR: «اعمل معنا»), the last item of the `base.ts` tree.

## Honesty

Only Eastgate has a documented before (31). The other four show today's measured median and never a derived before. Old-side map labels show a minute figure only for Eastgate. Hartley Heights has no case study in the content and says so. The caption states it on every beat.

## Why the other six grammars lost (and the filmic one-shot)

The comparison is the argument, so two states must be visible at once. Filmic one-shot and continuous world need assets that do not exist. Chaptered editorial would show Today and With the toolkit one after the other. Live surface is close (the divider is a control) but the content has no state to compute. Typographic poster and gallery have no single claim or object collection.
